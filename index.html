The research

They went to church every week. They could not explain what Jesus died for.

Christian Smith, a sociologist at the University of Notre Dame, led the National Study of Youth and Religion, the most comprehensive investigation of American teenage religious life ever undertaken. His team surveyed more than 3,000 teenagers, then sat down for in-depth interviews to learn what they actually believed.

Most still called themselves Christian. Most could not describe sin, repentance, or why the cross matters. Yet across every denomination and region they shared one consistent creed.

The teenagers did not invent it. They learned it from their parents, their pastors and their churches.

Published in Soul Searching: The Religious and Spiritual Lives of American Teenagers (Smith & Denton, 2005).

Its name

Moralistic Therapeutic Deism

Each word is deliberate. Together they describe a faith that feels familiar enough that almost no one notices what has been lost.

Moralistic

Religion is mainly about being good, nice and fair.

Therapeutic

Religion exists to help you feel better about yourself.

Deism

God exists, but stays out of the way unless you need Him.

The counterfeit creed

Five beliefs you have probably heard from a pulpit

Each sounds Christian. Set beside the Scriptures, each one is something else.

1
What MTD says

A God exists who created the world and watches over human life.

What the gospel says

The God of the Bible is not a distant watchman. He speaks, acts, judges and saves, and He makes real demands on His people.

2
What MTD says

God wants people to be good, nice and fair, as most world religions teach.

What the gospel says

God does not merely ask us to be nice. He calls us to be holy, and His commands are grounded in who He is and what He has done in Christ.

3
What MTD says

The central goal of life is to be happy and feel good about oneself.

What the gospel says

The first word of Jesus' public ministry was not "be happy." It was "repent." Our chief end is to glorify God and enjoy Him forever.

4
What MTD says

God only needs to be involved when there is a problem to solve.

What the gospel says

God is not an emergency contact. "Love the Lord your God with all your heart" makes Him the center of every ordinary day.

5
What MTD says

Good people go to heaven when they die.

What the gospel says

All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God. Salvation is the free gift of God in Christ Jesus, not a reward for being good enough.

It is not a rejection of Christianity. It is a replacement of it: a counterfeit close enough to the original that most people in most churches cannot tell the difference.

Not in the pews. In the pulpit.

How it got in

It came through the front door

Removing barriers became removing content

The seeker-sensitive movement rightly wanted church to be welcoming. Over time, hell, sin, the exclusivity of Christ and the cost of discipleship were softened or dropped. What remained was not a more accessible Christianity.

The therapeutic culture moved into the sanctuary

From the 1970s on, the language of self-help became the language of sermons. The message began to sound like the culture's, with a Jesus overlay on top.

It produces no martyrs and no missionaries

No one risks a labor camp for the belief that God wants them to be happy. A faith that costs nothing sustains nothing, which is why so many have quietly walked away.

A warning given in 1973

Christianity in Crisis

Rev. Sun Myung Moon · October 1, 1973
“God is leaving America now!”

Three decades before sociologists named it, Rev. Moon warned that American Christianity had turned self-centered, focused on personal salvation and comfort rather than God's will for the world.

  • Churches divided by denomination and race, becoming venues for social functions.
  • Materialism and pragmatism putting profit ahead of spiritual values.
  • A “me generation” of young people caught in selfish love and broken families.
  • A call for a new, self-sacrificing movement of young people to revive America's spirit.

New hope for Christianity

The New Future of Christianity

Rev. Sun Myung Moon · September 18, 1974 · Madison Square Garden, New York
“The new hope for mankind is [the return of] the Messiah.”

A year later, before a packed Madison Square Garden, the warning turned to hope: Christianity's future lies not in comfort but in living for God's will and the salvation of the whole world.

  • With hundreds of denominations reading one Bible, believers need God's own perspective, not only human interpretation.
  • Christians are called beyond personal salvation to help save the world.
  • America has a providential role, as Israel and Rome once did, in preparing for Christ's return.
  • Believers must stay open and ready to recognize the Lord when He comes.
